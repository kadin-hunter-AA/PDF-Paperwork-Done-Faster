Harbor District Redevelopment · Draft EIR3 Hydrology and Water Quality
3.2 Stormwater Management

The project site drains to three outfalls along the eastern seawall. Under existing conditions, runoff from approximately 41.6 acres of impervious surface enters the harbor without treatment. The proposed drainage plan routes flows through bioretention cells in the public open space and two underground detention vaults before discharge.

Peak runoff for the 25-year, 24-hour storm event is estimated at 186 cubic feet per second (cfs) under existing conditions, based on rainfall depths from the 2019 county design manual. With the proposed improvements, peak discharge would decrease to 143 cfs, a reduction of approximately 23 percent (Table 3.2-1).

Table 3.2-1 Peak Flow by Outfall, 25-Year Storm
OutfallArea (ac)Existing (cfs)Proposed (cfs)Change
OF-1 Pier Street14.26851−25%
OF-2 Marina Basin18.98263−23%
OF-3 Seawall South8.53629−19%
Total41.6186143−23%
Source: Kessler Civil Engineering, Preliminary Drainage Report, 2026.
3.2.1 Water Quality Treatment

Bioretention cells are sized to capture and treat runoff from the 85th-percentile, 24-hour storm, consistent with the Municipal Regional Stormwater Permit. Treated flows would pass through engineered soil media before entering the subdrain system. Pollutants of concern include total suspended solids, copper, zinc, and petroleum hydrocarbons associated with vehicle traffic.

Detention vaults are designed for a 72-hour drawdown period. Maintenance access would be provided from Pier Street, and inspection reports would be submitted annually to the Public Works Department as a condition of approval (Mitigation Measure HYD-2).

Impact HYD-1: Construction and operation of the project would not violate water quality standards or waste discharge requirements. With implementation of Mitigation Measures HYD-1 and HYD-2, the impact would be less than significant.
